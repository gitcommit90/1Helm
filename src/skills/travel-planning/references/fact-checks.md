# Airport, border and transfer facts

Use this when a plan depends on an airport, terminal, border or customs area, an entry or transit rule, a connection, a
transfer or a fast-track service. Getting one of these wrong strands people, so each claim needs the source that owns it.

## Treat every leg on its own

For each leg that matters, note:

- origin and destination: airport code, city, country or territory, and border or customs area;
- local departure and arrival dates and times, when known;
- the operating airline and flight number, when known;
- the airline's or authority's own classification (domestic, international, precleared), when published;
- the terminal, with where you read it and when;
- what follows the leg: immigration, customs, bag reclaim, transfer desk, connection, check-in.

Classify each leg from its own endpoints. Don't copy "domestic", "international", "airside", "precleared" or a terminal from
the leg before or after it. Different countries at the two ends means international unless an official source proves a
special arrangement for that route. The same country at both ends still needs a check for a special border or customs
regime (some territories have their own rules) before you call it domestic.

## Eligibility uses the minimum

Personal entry or transit advice rests only on: citizenship, the passport's issuing country and type, residency or visa
status, purpose, and length of stay or transit. If one of those is missing, give the general process and say the personal
answer is open; don't infer it from the itinerary. Never ask for, or keep, a passport number while planning.

## Match each claim to its owner

- Entry, transit, visa, arrival forms, passports, customs: the government's immigration or border authority.
- Terminal, connection, baggage, transfer desk, check-in: the operating airline or the airport.
- Fast-track eligibility, hours, meeting point, inclusions, price: the airport or the service's operator.
- Ground transfer time: a current route or timetable you looked up.
- Marketplaces and traveller reports: colour only, never the sole proof of a rule or an official service.

Check each rule for the travel date, and note the source, its link and when you read it. With no official source, mark the
claim unverified and make any recommendation that depends on it conditional.

Queue times: never present a remembered or typical wait as today's fact. If there's no official live wait source, label any
figure as historical or anecdotal. A fast-track recommendation rests on the verified arrival process, the person's limits
and the service's published terms.

## Advice is not purchase

Keep the analysis and recommendation here. When they want to buy a transfer or service, go through the handoff reference
with each leg, the eligibility facts and the sources attached. Compare only transfer options that serve the exact airport,
terminal, arrival time, party, luggage and destination. If nothing here can complete the booking, it's an item for the
person on the open list.
