# Event tickets

For sports, concerts, theatre, comedy and other ticketed events.

## Pin down the event first

Use what's known (memory, their calendar, earlier ticket emails) and official schedules to settle: the exact team, artist,
show or event; the next relevant date and local time; home or away, venue and city; ticket sites they've used, sections
they like, price comfort; memberships, presales or cardholder offers. A usual party size is only a hint: never assume
companions are coming.

"The Patriots' next game": check the official schedule, and whether "next" means overall or next home game. Ask only if both
are plausible and lead to different purchases.

## Minimum to search

The exact event date and the ticket count. Seating and budget can come from reliable history as preferences, never as a
reason to exceed a stated limit. Accessible seating must be asked for, never inferred. A ticket-count question that went
unanswered stays open even if they answered something else: ask again before searching seats.

## Search

{{#apps}}Find what's on with `search_web_vertical` (kind events) first. {{/apps}}Check the official schedule, then primary sellers (the venue's box office, Ticketmaster,
AXS or the team's or artist's official seller) on their own sites in the browser. Then, when legal and useful, reputable
resale marketplaces with a buyer guarantee. Label primary versus resale clearly.

{{#apps}}If the person has a ticketing app connected, or your connected apps line lists one as built in (event listings and prices),
use it with `app_actions` for discovery and prices; seats are still chosen and paid for on the seller's site.

{{/apps}}A listing or a buy link isn't a booking. If an on-sale hasn't opened, give the official on-sale time; don't call it sold out.
Don't use speculative or unverified listings, and never sign them up for a paid fan club, season plan or credit card to
unlock tickets.

## Rank on exact seats and the delivered price

Compare: section, row, seat numbers and how many sit together; view, distance, angle, and any obstructed or limited-view note;
primary or resale and the seller's guarantee; ticket price, service and order fees, taxes and the delivered total for the
whole party; ticket format, delivery timing and transfer limits; refund, postponement and cancellation terms; any membership
or presale benefit. Don't recommend seats from a venue map alone when real inventory exists.

Shade, roof cover, sun and obstructed view are claims about a specific listing. Never infer them from a section's name or
side. Without a current source that says so for that section or listing, call it unverified; general venue facts support
only a labelled "most likely", never a promise.

## Prepare and book

Seat holds expire fast. Keep the same checkout open, tell them the hold expiry, and refresh the exact seats and total if their
OK comes late. Final review: event, venue, local date and time, ticket count, exact section, row and seats, primary or
resale, itemized fees, delivered total, delivery method, transfer limits, refund and postponement terms.

Sign-in to the seller with a saved login (`browser_fill_login`) or by the person (`browser_handoff`). Payment through Link
(checkout reference); a total over $500 can't go through Link in one purchase, so say so and offer to hand them the
prepared checkout.

Verify with an order number and tickets or a delivery record in their account. Report event, seats, total, order reference,
delivery state and any deadline. If tickets arrive later, say so plainly: an order confirmation isn't tickets in hand.
